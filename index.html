Research

Detecting deepfakes in online content

Generated and face-swapped media now spreads through social networks faster than people can check it. My research builds detectors that are accurate, and whose decisions can be explained.

The problem

Deepfakes are images and videos where a face has been generated or swapped by a neural network. They are used for fake profiles, fraud, impersonation and misinformation. They also threaten identity checks that rely on a selfie or a video call.

My approach

I build on Inception-style convolutional networks. An Inception block looks at an image through several filter sizes at the same time. Small filters pick up fine traces such as blending edges, texture and noise. Larger filters see wider inconsistencies across the face.

Across my doctoral work I developed three variants of a Deep Inception CNN. They study how to learn better representations of deepfake content, how to supervise the network's internal features, and how to combine these ideas into one integrated model for online content.

Why it matters in the Gulf

Banks, telecoms, newsrooms and government digital services all verify images and identities at scale. Reliable, explainable deepfake detection supports trust in that digital infrastructure.

Question 1

Which visual traces separate a real face from a generated or swapped one?

Question 2

How can a network learn those traces so they still work on new content from social media?

Question 3

How do we show people why a detector flagged an image, so its output can be trusted and checked?

Interactive lab

See what a deepfake detector sees

Image generators often enlarge pictures step by step, and that can leave a faint, regular grid that people cannot see. This lab breaks an image into the signals an Inception block works with, and shows that grid in the frequency spectrum. Compare the two samples, or upload your own image.

Your image is analysed in your browser and never uploaded.
Grid-peak score—

Analysing…

How strongly the image repeats on a regular two-pixel grid, compared with its normal fine detail.

An educational tool, not a deepfake detector. The grid-peak score measures one known trace. Real photos that were resized or heavily compressed can also show peaks, and many modern generators leave none. A trained detector combines thousands of traces like this one.
